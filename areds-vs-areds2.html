The original AREDS formula

The original AREDS formula was a major step in AMD nutrition research because it gave clinicians a studied supplement framework rather than vague eye-vitamin claims. The original formula included vitamin C, vitamin E, beta-carotene, zinc, and copper.

That formula is still important because it explains why zinc, copper, and antioxidant vitamins appear on so many current labels. It also explains why some shoppers still see references to the original high-zinc approach when comparing products.

Historical role

Original AREDS is best viewed as the foundation. AREDS2 is the later update that shaped modern product comparisons.

The AREDS2 update

AREDS2 evaluated changes to the original formula. The most recognizable change was replacing beta-carotene with lutein and zeaxanthin. These carotenoids are now central to modern AREDS2-style products.

The update matters because many consumers assume every eye supplement is similar. In reality, a general eye-health product may not match the AREDS2-style ingredient pattern. A careful comparison requires reading the Supplement Facts label.

Modern formula pattern

Look for vitamin C, vitamin E, lutein, zeaxanthin, zinc, and copper when evaluating an AREDS2-style supplement.

Beta-carotene and smoker considerations

Beta-carotene is one of the most important differences between AREDS and AREDS2. The original formula included beta-carotene, while AREDS2-style formulas use lutein and zeaxanthin instead.

This distinction is especially important for smokers and former smokers to discuss with an eye-care professional. Patients should not assume an older formula is appropriate simply because it is called an eye vitamin. If you smoke now, smoked in the past, or are unsure which formula you have, review the exact label with your clinician or pharmacist.

Zinc differences

Zinc remains a key ingredient in the AREDS and AREDS2 conversation, but product labels can differ. Some products use the original 80 mg zinc approach, while others use lower-zinc versions. This is one reason the phrase AREDS2 is not enough by itself; the actual label matters.

On this site, Generic4Me is positioned as an original 80 mg zinc value option. Viteyes is positioned as a lower-zinc premium option. PreserVision is treated as a widely recognized benchmark that many shoppers use for comparison.

What this means when shopping

When comparing AREDS and AREDS2 products, do not rely only on the front of the bottle. Check whether the product contains beta-carotene, lutein, zeaxanthin, zinc, copper, vitamin C, and vitamin E. Then compare serving size and cost per day.

A good shopping process starts with the medical recommendation. Ask whether AREDS2 is appropriate for your AMD stage, whether beta-carotene should be avoided, and whether a high-zinc or lower-zinc product is preferred.

FAQ

Is AREDS2 the newer formula?

Yes. AREDS2 is commonly used as the updated formula framework for current product comparisons. The main practical change is that AREDS2-style products use lutein and zeaxanthin instead of beta-carotene. That difference matters because patients, especially smokers and former smokers, should know which formula they are taking. Even with AREDS2 products, labels can vary by zinc level, serving size, and cost per day.

Does AREDS2 replace eye care?

No. AREDS2 does not replace eye exams, retinal monitoring, injections, imaging, or any treatment recommended by an ophthalmologist, optometrist, or retina specialist. It is a supplement formula discussed in a specific AMD context. Patients should continue regular eye care and should report vision changes promptly.

Why did AREDS2 remove beta-carotene?

AREDS2 evaluated lutein and zeaxanthin as alternatives to beta-carotene. This change became important because beta-carotene supplementation raised concerns for smokers and former smokers in other research. The practical takeaway is that anyone with a current or past smoking history should read labels carefully and discuss the right formula with a clinician.

Are AREDS and AREDS2 products interchangeable?

Not always. The names are similar, but the formulas are not identical. A product based on the original AREDS formula may contain beta-carotene, while an AREDS2-style product should use lutein and zeaxanthin. Before switching products, compare the Supplement Facts panel and ask your eye-care professional whether the change is appropriate.
